# Gate

- **Mandatory class**: none
- **Depth**: standard (a read-only experiment and standalone scorer; no production review, merge-gate or release decision changes)
- **Risk tier**: product
- **Author's risk assessment**: Re-tiered from `internal-tooling` to `product` after F2 wave 3: the measurement needed a third fix round, which `internal-tooling` caps at two; the reach of the change is unchanged and every wave reported `security_delta: no`. Wrong joins, duplicated targets, inherited historical matches or mistaking unreported targets for false drops can produce a false recommendation and waste bounded experiment spend. No scorer output authorizes apply, changes model defaults or gates a release. Historical repositories are read-only, raw sessions/plans stay local and only reviewed sanitized normalized evidence is published. Routine provider authentication is unchanged; the tool never reads credentials. The normal experimental call budget is bounded in design D5; this is proof tooling, not a money-transfer or billing mechanism.
- **Whole-diff review**: not needed (one standalone scorer with its contract tests and the experiment/result artifacts it reads; no third independently testable runtime layer)
- **Accepted residual risks**: Five selected plans and repeated trials do not establish population recall or model equivalence; historical and fresh arms differ in brief, policy and era, so historical comparisons are confounded. Missing historical timing or unresolvable source provenance stays explicitly unavailable. Blinding hides identity metadata but cannot guarantee style anonymity. No full fix-cycle savings are measured. These limitations must remain visible and do not excuse incorrect joins, hidden missing cells or unsupported causal claims.
- **Out of scope for blocking**: Changing the production review loop, model-role configuration, runtime fallback implementation, the parked metaharness replay benchmark, risky-depth reviewer execution and full historical fix-cycle replay. Findings in those surfaces are follow-ups unless this change modifies them or relies on an unsafe result from them. Publication privacy, correct measured counts, input integrity and real experiment execution remain in scope.
