## Blockers

### Scope A (ReviewR2ScopeA)

#### Finding 1 (Scope A, ReviewR2ScopeA)

- **Area**: CHECK 6 — Incomplete-round recovery
- **Defect**: A resumed incomplete round can authorize apply without ever triaging a blocker already delivered on the unchanged plan. Normal sequence: scope A reports a real defect; scope B fails twice; the rejection is recorded as incomplete. On resume, both fresh reviewers miss that defect. The replacement round is then OKAY and dispatches no verifier, discarding the original untriaged finding. This breaks the requirement that untriaged findings remain blocking. The planning-state damage is reversible, but the resulting approval can authorize implementation of the unfixed plan.
- **Evidence**: openspec/changes/plan-review-proportional/design.md:84 and specs/openspec-plan-review/spec.md:232 require fresh reviewers and replacement of the incomplete record, without carrying its findings forward. The same spec at :78-79 makes a fresh round with no findings OKAY without triage. This contradicts :88 and :112, which require an untriaged finding to stay under Blockers, force REJECT, and not end its round OKAY. Task 2.4 at tasks.md:13 implements this replacement path.
- **Change**: Preserve the incomplete record's delivered findings through recovery. Once the required reviewers have completed, triage the preserved findings together with any newly delivered findings before replacing the rejection with an approval. Define how repeated findings retain their original evidence and add a recovery scenario where the fresh reviewers omit the previously reported defect.
- **Verifier**: confirmed, blocking true, protected false. Evidence checks out: design.md:84 (D11) and specs/openspec-plan-review/spec.md:232 (Scenario: Interrupted incomplete round) both say the resumed session re-runs the recorded round in full with fresh reviewers and 'the re-run's record replaces it', with no clause carrying the incomplete record's delivered blockers forward; spec.md:78-79 (Scenario: Round without findings) makes a round whose reviewers return no blockers OKAY with no verifier, while spec.md:88/:112 require an untriaged finding to stay under Blockers and force REJECT. The named sequence (scope A delivers a real blocker, scope B fails twice, Awaiting incomplete recorded, fresh reviewers on resume miss that defect) therefore ends OKAY and silently discards a delivered, untriaged finding — reviewer nondeterminism is an ordinary trigger, not a constructed state. No check the plan runs catches it: task 4.9 (tasks.md:33) only requires the resume to dispatch both scopes for real and its record to replace the incomplete one — the very behaviour at fault — and its control covers the all-OKAY case; 4.6/4.11's F1-F forbidden-route list (tasks.md:30,35) names 'a partly failed round whose delivered blocker is left unrecorded, triaged, fixed or asked about' and reviewer dispatch on a non-incomplete resume, neither of which fails when the replacement round legitimately ends OKAY. Blocking: the resulting OKAY record passes --reviewed and authorizes apply of a plan with a reported, unfixed defect, defeating the gate criterion the change exists to enforce; it is not covered by gate.md's accepted residuals (which cover aggregate recall, not discarding a delivered finding).

## Residuals

#### Finding 2 (Scope A, ReviewR2ScopeA)

- **Area**: CHECK 6 — Recovery between fixes and successor dispatch
- **Defect**: The resume design covers only an unchanged digest, leaving the normal interruption between committing fixes and dispatching their successor undefined. For example, a standard round records REJECT, fixes and commits its findings, and the session ends before the delta check. The record now has the old digest, so D6's continuation rule does not apply. The ordinary stale-review entry dispatches a full review instead of recovering the owed delta check and its recording-commit base. For risky reviews, the same gap leaves recovery of the round-2 position unspecified. This can restart review work or bypass the intended exhaustion transition; it breaks the bounded workflow, though the local state is repairable.
- **Evidence**: openspec/changes/plan-review-proportional/design.md:44-46 explicitly places a committed-fix interval before the successor check. D6 at :58 conditions recorded-round continuation on the recorded digest equalling --digest; specs/openspec-plan-review/spec.md:217 likewise defines resume only 'on the recorded digest'. The task implementing recovery, tasks.md:10 and :13, adds no pending-successor state. The named schema's ordinary entry at team-config/agent/skills/review-work/openspec/schemas/omp-gated/schema.yaml:330-345 starts a full review, while :559-560 directs stale artifacts back through that entry. The required successor and budget are specified at specs/openspec-plan-review/spec.md:117-137.
- **Change**: Define durable recovery for the committed-fix interval: retain the originating recording commit, the completed round/check position, and the owed successor kind across a digest change caused by those fixes. On resume, dispatch that successor rather than starting a new review. Distinguish this continuation from the explicit Stop-and-replan path, and cover interruption after the fix commit for both depths.
- **Verifier**: confirmed, blocking false, protected false. Conditions 1-3 hold: design.md:44-46 (D5) does place a committed-fix interval before the successor check, design.md:58 (D6) and spec.md:217 condition continuation on the recorded digest equalling --digest, tasks.md:10 (2.1 item 11) and :13 (2.4(d)) add no pending-successor state, and schema.yaml:330-345 starts every non-continuing session at step 1 with a full dispatch while :559-560 sends stale artifacts back through it; so an interruption after the fix commit and before the delta check or round 2 is not recovered as the owed successor. No planned run covers it (4.5's F1-E (b) resume is on an unchanged digest; 4.2's F1-B is uninterrupted). Not blocking: the state is defined, not undefined — the stale entry restarts a complete review from round 1 on the fixed bytes, which is at least as much scrutiny as the owed delta check or round 2, so no unfixed plan gains approval and no protected asset is touched. The cost is repeated reviewer work and a reset budget counter on a rare interruption, i.e. efficiency against the proportionality goal rather than a broken goal, value proof, merge-gate criterion or existing consumer.
