## Blockers

### Correctness (StopCorrectness2)

1. **Correctness — incompatible eligibility rules** — The specification simultaneously requires eligibility to be independent of the lens and requires every blocker raised by a security lens or a security_delta: yes trailer to be ineligible. The tasks also omit that latter exclusion. Evidence: openspec/changes/review-stop-rule/specs/review-blocker-disposition/spec.md:11 forbids deriving eligibility from lens identity; :25 makes that identity decisive. tasks.md:7-8 name security and privacy paths but not every blocker raised by those reviewers. For example, a security reviewer reporting an otherwise below-threshold stale cross-reference must be both eligible and ineligible. The conflict blocks a conforming implementation; its effects on a review decision are reversible before release. Change: Decide whether reviewer identity is an independent exclusion or the stated consequence alone governs; reconcile both requirements and carry the decision into both implementation tasks.
2. **Correctness — existing override paths bypass ineligibility** — The plan says certain blockers must not be accepted, but retains existing override and freeze paths that can accept them without applying the new eligibility rule. Evidence: openspec/changes/review-stop-rule/specs/review-blocker-disposition/spec.md:25-40 prohibits recording specified blockers as accepted risks, while tasks.md:7 preserves the existing Override path and specs/openspec-plan-review/spec.md:31 preserves post-implementation convergence. team-config/agent/skills/review-work/openspec/schemas/omp-gated/schema.yaml:406-416 offers Accept and Override after a final REJECT; team-config/agent/skills/review-work/SKILL.md:424-425,436 permits freeze or delta acceptance. A normal final round containing an authorization blocker can therefore still be accepted rather than fixed; if released, the resulting defect need not be reversible. Change: Specify how eligibility constrains every retained Accept, Override, freeze and delta-accept path, or explicitly revise the requirements and the intended safety guarantee.
3. **Architecture — F3 and delta acceptance use different records** — The design assumes F3 and delta accepted blockers are recorded using F2 override and downgrades, but their existing acceptance paths use different artifacts and are not changed by the plan. Evidence: openspec/changes/review-stop-rule/design.md:35 and tasks.md:8 claim all post-implementation accepted blockers use override plus downgrades. team-config/agent/skills/integration-review/SKILL.md:147 records frozen F3 Must-fix classes as follow-ups in the F3 report, while team-config/agent/skills/review-work/SKILL.md:436 records delta acceptance with GC accept. A frozen F3 finding or accepted delta need not appear as a FAIL trailer in the F2 gate record, so the promised disposition and record are not produced. The missing record is reversible while the gate remains open. Change: Design the eligibility decision and durable disposition for F3 freeze and delta acceptance at their actual entry points, and reconcile the requirement with the existing record vocabulary and no-tooling constraint.

### Verification (StopVerification2)

1. **Quality and verification — task 2.1 evidence oracle** — The F1 Verify command accepts any allowed decision as a row's expected result, rather than binding each row name to the expected decision specified by the task. An incorrect decision can therefore pass by recording it as both expected and observed, with passed=true. This makes the final value proof unreliable; the evidence can be corrected, but an incorrectly accepted gate can allow an irreversible released defect. Evidence: openspec/changes/review-stop-rule/tasks.md:14 lists fixed name-to-decision expectations, but its command checks only V.includes(x.expected) and x.observed===x.expected, plus self-reported passed and an artifact hash. It never checks the declared mapping or verifies that the artifact contains the quoted instruction, source and observed decision. Change: Bind each row name to its prescribed expected result in the verifier and check the cited evidence against that row's actual source, instruction and observed result, so an incorrect decision cannot pass by changing the matrix.
2. **Quality and verification — requirements and negative controls** — The planned replay cases do not discriminate several required behaviors. Ordinary instruction edits could omit or weaken these behaviors while all twelve named F1 rows still pass. The resulting review or exemption can incorrectly accept a blocker or skip a required delta review; the instruction is reversible, but an admitted released defect need not be. Evidence: tasks.md:14 covers one eligible round, one blocking round, credential, missing verdict, unstated consequence, checker, authorization, three commit cases, F2 and installation. It does not exercise: deriving eligibility from severity/lens or another predicate and the security-lens/security_delta and mandatory-class exclusions (specs/review-blocker-disposition/spec.md:10-35); ambiguous/mixed consequences (lines 44-53); generated documentation or compiler/linter/build directives and recording the exemption reason (lines 72-87); per-blocker Fix/Accept/Stop, stated consequences, the user's recorded reason and fresh round, or the third-REJECT path and mixed-blocker question (specs/openspec-plan-review/spec.md:5-30); nor the F3 and delta lanes' lack of an early ask and their recorded downgrades (lines 32-42). Design.md:35 claims each requirement and negative control is covered, and design.md:31 claims F3 inheritance is verified, but task 2.1 names no such F3 case. Change: Add discriminating replay or instruction-review cases for each omitted scenario and negative control, including F3 and delta inheritance, and require evidence of each expected effect rather than only a coarse decision label.
3. **Executability and verification — tasks 1.1 and 1.2** — The named Verify commands can pass when the instruction work in their own task is absent or broken. Normal task execution may therefore mark a broken instruction change complete; the later F1 cases do not cover all its requirements. This creates a reversible local defect and can undermine the release gate. Evidence: tasks.md:7 uses OpenSpec and change-folder validators; validate-openspec-change.mjs:311-321 reads proposal, gate, tasks and specs rather than the edited schema instruction. tasks.md:8 runs gate-record, convergence and installer tests; scripts/install-team-config.test.ts:13-21 constructs a temporary repository from supplied fixture files, so its installer tests do not exercise the edited shipped instruction files. These commands do not discriminate the behavior required by their respective tasks. Change: Give each task a verification that exercises its edited instruction against representative positive and negative scenarios, or explicitly run the corresponding discriminating F1 cases before marking that task complete.

### Checks (StopChecks2c)

1. **CHECK 4 — eligibility decision** — The requirements give incompatible results for the same blocker: eligibility must not depend on lens identity, yet every blocker raised by the security lens must be ineligible. The same conflict applies to the additional security_delta rule versus a decision based only on the blocker’s consequence. This leaves the gate decision undecidable for an otherwise eligible finding from that lens. Evidence: specs/review-blocker-disposition/spec.md:12-22 forbids deriving eligibility from lens identity or a separate predicate; lines 24-26 require every blocker raised by a security lens or security_delta: yes trailer to be ineligible. In an ordinary review, a checks lens can report an evidence-bookkeeping blocker with no protected asset or consumer in its path; the two rules prescribe opposite outcomes. Incorrect acceptance could weaken review; an incorrect refusal causes reversible extra review work. Change: Choose and state an unambiguous precedence for these exceptions, reconcile the negative control, and add a control using an otherwise eligible blocker from the security lens and one from a security_delta: yes trailer.
2. **CHECK 6 — post-implementation entry-path parity** — The F3 disposition decision is not covered by the planned edit: F3 handles its own FAIL and freeze decision before the referenced review-work step 5 is reached. Consequently an F3 security or other non-eligible blocker can still be frozen as a follow-up, contrary to the new cross-lane requirement. F1 tests only an F2 eligible blocker and cannot expose this path. Evidence: design.md:35 and tasks.md:7-14 rely on review-work Fix waves step 5 for F3 and test only f2-eligible-blocker. team-config/agent/skills/integration-review/SKILL.md:140-147 makes the F3 STOP/freeze decision in its own Step 4; lines 159-164 invoke review-work step 5 only after a cleanup commit. specs/review-blocker-disposition/spec.md:24-26 requires security and gate blockers never to be eligible. Under a normal F3 FAIL followed by STOP, the existing freeze path can leave such a blocker as a follow-up; a defect admitted to release can be irreversible. Change: Apply the disposition rule at the actual F3 decision point, inventory plan-review, F2, F3 and delta paths explicitly, and add an F3-specific negative control exercising freeze with a non-eligible blocker.
